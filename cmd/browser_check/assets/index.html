A zero request does not mean zero actual delay. The browser may keep more video to handle irregular packet arrival. An accepted value only confirms the request was stored. The measured buffer time below tells us what happened.

After both videos arrive, the page waits 10 seconds, measures for about 30 seconds, then closes both connections. Run again with the left/right positions swapped. A short run cannot establish reliability.

“Buffer time” is time spent in the browser's compressed-video buffer. “Last packet to expected display” is an optional browser estimate. Neither is camera-to-screen delay. For that, compare the white clock with its filmed number. These counters cannot prove that every picture is intact.

Both audio and video receivers get the zero request when supported, because their synchronization can affect the video delay. Both players are muted. Dropped frames, freezes and decoder counters describe symptoms; missing statistics are shown as unavailable.
